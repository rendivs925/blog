## PHYSICS — The Fundamental Questions

### 1. What is dark matter?
About 85% of all matter in the universe is invisible. We see its gravity but we cannot detect it. We do not know what it is made of.

### 2. What is dark energy?
About 68% of the universe's energy is causing the expansion of the universe to accelerate. We have no idea what it is.

### 3. The cosmological constant problem
Quantum field theory predicts the vacuum energy to be 10^120 times larger than what we observe. This is the worst prediction in the history of physics. Something cancels it out and we do not know what.

### 4. Why is there more matter than antimatter?
The Big Bang should have created equal amounts of matter and antimatter. They should have annihilated each other completely. Yet we exist. Something created an imbalance and we do not know what.

### 5. What happened before the Big Bang?
General relativity breaks down at the singularity. We cannot see past the moment of creation. What caused the conditions for existence to arise?

### 6. The measurement problem in quantum mechanics
A particle exists in multiple states at once until it is measured. What constitutes a measurement? Why does observation collapse the wavefunction? Does reality exist without an observer?

### 7. The arrow of time
The laws of physics work the same forward and backward. Yet time only moves forward. Why did the universe start with such low entropy? Why does time have a direction?

### 8. The black hole information paradox
When matter falls into a black hole and the black hole evaporates, the information about that matter seems to be lost forever. Quantum mechanics says information cannot be destroyed. Which theory is wrong?

### 9. Are there extra dimensions?
String theory requires 10 or 11 dimensions. Why do we only see four? Where are the others? Can we ever detect them?

### 10. The hierarchy problem
Gravity is 10^32 times weaker than the electromagnetic force. Why is gravity so incredibly weak compared to everything else?

### 11. Origin of cosmic magnetic fields
Magnetic fields exist everywhere in the universe from galaxies to galaxy clusters. Where did they come from?

### 12. The Hubble tension
The universe appears to be expanding at two different rates depending on how you measure it. One measurement gives one value and another gives a different value. They disagree by 5.7 sigma. Something is wrong with our model.

### 13. The fine-tuning problem
The fundamental constants of physics appear to be precisely tuned for life to exist. If any constant were slightly different, stars would not form, atoms would not hold together, and life would be impossible. Is this coincidence or is there a deeper reason?

### 14. Theory of Everything
Can quantum mechanics and general relativity be unified into a single consistent theory? Is spacetime fundamentally continuous or discrete? What is the nature of quantum gravity?

## NEUROSCIENCE — The Mind and Consciousness

### 15. What is consciousness?
What is the neural basis of subjective experience? Why does a collection of neurons produce the feeling of being alive? Why is there something it feels like to be you?

### 16. The hard problem of consciousness
We can explain how the brain processes information but we cannot explain why there is subjective experience at all. Why does all this neural activity feel like something from the inside?

### 17. The binding problem
The brain processes color in one area, motion in another, sound in another, and memory in another. How are all these separate pieces combined into a single unified experience?

### 18. Where are memories stored and how are they retrieved?
We know memories involve changes in synaptic connections but we do not understand how a specific memory is encoded, stored for decades, and retrieved instantly.

### 19. What is the biological function of sleep and dreams?
We spend a third of our lives asleep and we do not know exactly why. Dreams remain one of the greatest mysteries in neuroscience.

### 20. Does free will exist?
Neuroscience experiments suggest our brains make decisions before we consciously become aware of them. What does this mean for the concept of free will?

### 21. How do general anesthetics work?
We use anesthetics every day in surgery. They can make a person completely unconscious yet we do not fully understand the mechanism by which they do so.

### 22. What is the neural basis of the self?
What makes you feel like you? Where does the sense of identity come from?

## BIOLOGY — Life Itself

### 23. How did life begin? (Abiogenesis)
How did non-living chemicals organize themselves into the first self-replicating living cell? This is perhaps the greatest scientific mystery of all. We have many hypotheses but no definitive answer.

### 24. How did the genetic code originate?
The genetic code is universal across all life on Earth. How did the mapping between DNA codons and amino acids arise? What was the molecular mechanism?

### 25. Why does aging happen?
Why do organisms deteriorate and die? There are many hypotheses but no unified theory of aging.

### 26. The protein folding problem
A protein is a chain of amino acids that folds into a specific three-dimensional shape. How does it find the correct shape among an astronomical number of possibilities in microseconds?

### 27. Why are amino acids left-handed and sugars right-handed? (Homochirality)
Life on Earth uses only left-handed amino acids and right-handed sugars. This is a fundamental feature of biochemistry and we do not know why.

### 28. Origin of sexual reproduction
Sexual reproduction is costly and inefficient. Why did it evolve? Why does it persist?

### 29. What is the origin of viruses?
Exactly how and when did different groups of viruses originate? Where did they come from?

## COSMOLOGY AND ASTRONOMY

### 30. What is the size and shape of the universe?
The observable universe is 93 billion light-years across. But what lies beyond? Is the universe infinite? What is its topology?

### 31. Is the universe homogeneous and isotropic at large scales?
The cosmological principle assumes the universe looks the same everywhere. Recent observations suggest large-scale anomalies that challenge this assumption.

### 32. What caused the solar cycle?
How does the Sun generate its periodically reversing large-scale magnetic field? What caused historical grand minima like the Maunder Minimum?

### 33. What powers fast radio bursts (FRBs)?
These are millisecond-duration radio pulses from distant galaxies. Dozens of models have been proposed but none are widely accepted.

### 34. How do supermassive black holes form so early in the universe?
Quasars with black holes of 10 billion solar masses exist when the universe was only a few hundred million years old. How did they grow so fast?

### 35. What powers ultra-high-energy cosmic rays?
Some cosmic rays have energies millions of times higher than anything produced by human particle accelerators. Where do they come from?

### 36. The coronal heating problem
Why is the Sun's corona (atmosphere) millions of degrees hotter than its surface?
